# Notes for the reviewer

Every file in this folder is a draft (`"review": {"status": "draft"}`)
until a reviewer approves it. Approval sets `status` to `approved` and
records `by` and `at`. The app publishes only approved files.

The drafting sessions flagged these lines for a careful look.

## All dates

- The Bible text is the World English Bible. The Lectionary sometimes
  cuts a verse in half (for example Isaiah 61:1-2a); the booklet prints
  the whole verse, and the guide restates it.
- No guide quotes a saint, pope or scholar.

## 2026-10-25

- The first reading's chunks were rewritten after a verse-numbering fix
  (Exodus 22:20-26 in the Lectionary is 22:21-27 in the World English
  Bible).
- Kids, first reading (22:23-24) now ends "that person's own wife and
  children will be left alone, with no husband and no father," which is
  what 22:24 says. The earlier draft added a reason the reading does not
  give.

## 2026-11-01, All Saints

- The plain intro names "the First Letter of John" rather than "his
  letter," so it does not claim that one John wrote both Revelation and
  1 John.

## 2026-11-08

- The Gospel's "virgins" are called "bridesmaids" in both editions.
- The plain box says the oil stands for a readiness no one can borrow.
- Both psalm boxes say he will praise God as long as he lives (63:4).
  The earlier "all day" was not in the psalm.

## 2026-11-22, Christ the King

- Reading 2 box: one line on 1 Corinthians 15:28, that the Son is not
  less than the Father and that the verse speaks of Christ, who became
  man, offering his finished work to the Father. Check the wording.
- Kids Ezekiel: "the fat, strong ones who pushed the others around."
  The "pushed around" detail comes from 34:21, outside the reading.

## 2026-11-29

- Isaiah: the World English Bible numbering skips 64:2 because the
  Lectionary jumps from 63:19b to 64:2. That is correct.

## 2026-12-08, Immaculate Conception

- Every edition says the feast is Mary's own conception free from
  original sin, by grace through her Son, and the Gospel boxes say the
  Annunciation is about Jesus' conception.
- Genesis 3:15 is explained as the first announcement of the Savior,
  with Mary as the new Eve, following CCC 410-411 (not quoted).
- The angel's greeting is rendered "full of grace."

## 2026-12-13, Gaudete

- John 1:19 "the Jews" is rendered "the Jewish leaders in Jerusalem"
  (plain) and "leaders in Jerusalem" (kids).
- Kids intro says "pink candle"; plain says "rose candle."
- Plain Reading 2 box: "Near the end of his first letter" (the letter
  runs to 5:28) and "probably the earliest," as the other Thessalonians
  boxes say.

## 2026-12-20

- Reading 2 is Romans 16:25-27 in the Lectionary; the World English
  Bible prints it as 14:24-26. The box avoids saying it closes the
  letter.
- 2 Samuel 7:14 (the "rod of men" line) is restated as discipline, as a
  parent disciplines a child.
- Kids text keeps the word "virgin" with no extra explanation.
- Plain Gospel box: "The Church calls this scene the Annunciation." The
  earlier draft called Mary's answer itself the Annunciation.

## 2026-12-25

- The Gospel is John's prologue. Check how "the Word" is explained for
  children.

## 2026-12-27, Holy Family

- Colossians 3:18 stays close to the text in plain ("Wives, be subject
  to your husbands") and reads "follow their husbands' lead" in kids.
  Both boxes set it inside the love Paul describes just before.
- Luke 2:36-37: Anna is "a widow for many years," which avoids choosing
  between the two readings of her age.
- Simeon is called old (plain intro and Gospel box, kids Gospel box and
  listen line). Luke gives Anna's age but not Simeon's; the tradition of
  an old Simeon rests on 2:26-29. Keep or soften.

## 2027-01-03, Epiphany

- The gifts are explained as "Christians have long seen" them: king,
  God, burial. No number or names for the Magi.

## Background facts used across the drafts

Wisdom written in the century or so before Christ; 1 Thessalonians
likely Paul's earliest letter; a talent about twenty years of a
laborer's wages; Ezekiel preached to the exiles in Babylon about 600
years before Christ; Sirach about 200 years before Christ; "Jesus"
means "the Lord saves"; Abba is the word for father in Jesus' language;
Cornelius among the first non-Jewish believers; untying sandals was a
servant's task.
