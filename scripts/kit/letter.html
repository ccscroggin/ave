Dear Father,

Thank you for meeting with me. I’m Clark Scroggin. I am building Ave, a free app that helps people pray the rosary and prepare for Mass, and I would like your help making sure it teaches the faith correctly.

What Ave does

  • Guides the rosary, bead by bead, on a phone.
  • Shows each day’s Mass readings, and what happens at Mass that day.
  • Prints a small missalette: the readings with a guide in everyday words, in three editions. Plain is for adults, Kids is for ages 6 to 11, and Family gives parents questions to talk through at home.

The guides are drafted with the help of an AI system, then checked by a person. Nothing appears in the app until a reviewer approves it. That reviewer is what I am asking for.

What I am asking

  1. Would you review the guides? Each month that is the Sundays and holy days in three editions, about 15 short texts, ready six weeks ahead. You can mark up paper or reply by email.
  2. Your advice on three questions. Is a priest’s review enough, or should we ask the diocese for an imprimatur? The app uses the World English Bible, which is in the public domain, so its words differ from what is read at Mass; I plan to ask the USCCB for a license to use the Lectionary text. And is there anything in these editions you would change?

Practice mode: open a Sunday, read each edition next to the reading, fix a word, and approve. Nothing is saved. The app itself is at ave-daily.vercel.app.

In this folder

Sample booklets for the First Sunday of Advent in each edition and layout, a sample Advent season booklet, and a page on how reviewing works with a sign-off form.

Ave is free and takes no money: no ads, no sales, no donations. Anyone may print the booklets, and people who want to give are pointed to Catholic charities. It keeps no information about the people who use it, is not affiliated with the USCCB, and does not speak for the Church. The code is open source.
